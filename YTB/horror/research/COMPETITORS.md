# Competitor study: animated horror Shorts (2026-11-24)

Data: YouTube Data API (channel and video numbers, titles, durations) and thumbnails. Sheets: `competitors_art_visual.jpg`,
`competitors_art_narrated.jpg`, `competitors_art_ours.jpg` (each video's views and length under it). Raw numbers: `competitors_videos.json`.
Not covered: spoken scripts (no transcripts: vidIQ out of credits, video downloader not allowed in this environment).
Script notes below come from titles, lengths, on-screen text and thumbnails, not from the narration itself.

## What wins

1. **Short.** Wordless hits run 4-25 s; narrated hits 27-60 s. Ours average 84 s, and the ones over 90 s never left the first test audience.
2. **One idea a video.** A single premise you can say in one line. No second location, no subplot.
3. **The scary thing is shown, big and early.** The creature or the wrong face fills the frame in the first second and in the thumbnail.
4. **Bright, readable first frame even in horror.** Strong colour, high contrast, one subject in the middle. Our first frames are dark rooms with a small figure.
5. **A familiar thing made wrong.** Santa, a snowman, a dog, an ice-cream shop, a school bus. Instantly understood, no setup needed.
6. **The same frame every time (a brand).** ParaHaunt's red card, Vinay's yellow bar, Bugs' big title, dsand's numbered caption. Viewers recognise the channel in the feed.
7. **Series.** Numbered episodes with one recurring character (dsand 1..125, Punctured Souls 1..14) bring people back.
8. **Known names are search fuel.** ParaHaunt went from 0 to 465K in six months only telling legends people already search for.
9. **Second-person rules as the hook.** "Don't X at midnight", "If X happens, do this": the viewer is in the story from word one.

## What we keep

- Haru as the recurring character (dsand proves one character can carry a channel).
- Clay: nobody big does clay horror Shorts (Lee Hardcastle does clay, but long, not Shorts). That is our gap.

## What to change (proposals)

- Narrated reels 35-50 s, wordless reels 10-20 s. Script budget about 90-110 words for 40 s.
- First frame: Haru or the thing large in frame, lit, readable on a phone. Dark is fine; muddy is not.
- One fixed first-frame card for the channel (title + Haru's face, same colours every time).
- Titles under 60 characters, a promise or a rule, not a sentence-long summary.
- Number the stories ("Haru #12") so they read as a series.
- Try three formats: Haru's Rules (second person, 40 s), Haru meets a known legend (40-50 s), a wordless 12 s clay loop.
