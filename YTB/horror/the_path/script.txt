THE PATH  (about 2:45, 9:16, English)
Rule: they can't come inside, they never leave the doors, they only take you if you step out.
Pink italic = anyone who isn't Haru.

HOOK
I walked my whole village to the mountains. I got there alone.

THE DOORS
On Tuesday morning, there was one outside every door on our street.
Taller than the door frame. Dark fur. Too many teeth for its mouth.
It didn't knock. It just stood there, breathing on the wood.
Mr. Hamid went out to feed his goats.  (scream, cut)  His door is still open.
After that, nobody went out. They don't come in. They don't leave. They just wait.

THE FEVER
On the fourth night, my sister's fever got worse.
SISTER: Haru... it's so hot.
The only medicine on our street was across the road, at old Fatima's.
She held the bottle up in her window. She wouldn't open her door. I didn't blame her.
So I opened mine.
It bent down until its teeth were next to my ear. It breathed in my hair.
Then it moved out of my way.
It had never moved for anyone.

THE HANDS
My sister's fever broke the next morning.
After that, I went out every day. Bread. Water. Medicine.
On the third day, Mrs. Amina grabbed my sleeve before I could pull away.
AMINA: Please. My daughter is two houses down.
We crossed together. Nothing touched her.
Soon the whole street knew. If you held on to Haru, you were safe.
People started visiting each other again. Holding my hand, my hood, my bag. Walking right past them.

THE MOUNTAIN
From our roof, you can see the village up on the mountain.
Every night, there was still smoke coming from their chimneys.
And every one of them stood facing our doors, with its back to the mountain. Like it couldn't look at it.
SISTER: If everyone holds on to you, we can all get up there.

THE PATH
We left before sunrise. Forty-one people in one line, each holding the shoulder in front.
My sister held the strap of my backpack.
When we passed our houses, the doorsteps were empty. I thought they had finally given up.
Nobody talked on the path. You could only hear our feet on the stones.
After a while, there were fewer feet.
I told myself people were tired. Falling behind.
Then I heard breathing between the trees. Big, slow breathing.
I looked back once.
SISTER: Don't stop. Keep walking.
So I did.
(footsteps thinning)  Twenty. Ten. Four.
Then just two. Mine, and hers.
Then her hand slipped off my strap.
They never needed to come inside. They just needed someone to walk us out.

ENDING
At the tree line, they stopped. All of them. Like they always knew where to stop.
(silence, wind, a tug on the backpack strap)
SISTER (whisper): Haru. Wait for me.
(black, loop to the hook shot)
