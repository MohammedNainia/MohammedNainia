BLACK SKY  (about 2:40, 9:16, English)
Rules: "It" lives in the black sky and listens. Whatever it takes, everyone forgets.
Speak about something it took, and it takes you too. All it leaves is a black shadow burned into the ground, like ash.
Sound motifs: the low groan above the roofs (it moving); Mum's humming (the sunshine song); the kitchen clock.
Pink italic = anyone who isn't Haru.

HOOK
I talked about the sun. Now nobody remembers my mother.

MORNING
I woke up at seven. Outside, the sky was black. No stars. No moon. Just black.
The streetlamps were on. People were walking to work like it was normal.
In the kitchen, Mum was making tea under the lamp.
I asked her where the sun was.
(the cup breaks)
She put her hand over my mouth. Her hand was shaking.
MUM (whispering): Don't. It hears.

THE TOWN
I asked my teacher. She went pale and sent me home.
I asked the baker. He closed his shop.
Everyone said the same thing. The sky has always been black.
But I remembered it. Warm on my face. Yellow through the curtains.
I couldn't stop thinking about it. So I couldn't stop talking about it.

KENJI
Kenji from next door found me behind the school.
KENJI (whispering): I remember it too. I dream about it.
We drew it on the wall in chalk. A big yellow circle.
That night, something moved above the roofs. A long, low sound. Like something heavy turning over.
In the morning, the drawing was gone. So was Kenji.
Where he'd stood by the wall, there was a shadow. Black. Burned into the stones, like ash.
People walked over it without looking down.
I knocked on his door. His mother opened it.
I asked if Kenji was home.
KENJI'S MOTHER: Who?
His room was a storeroom. Boxes. Dust. Like he'd never lived there.

THE RULE
That's when I understood. It doesn't just take you. It takes you out of everyone.
That's why nobody remembers the sun.
Somebody talked about it. And it was taken.

MUM
I stopped talking. I stopped drawing. I said what everyone says.
The sky has always been black.
But that night, Mum sat on the edge of my bed. She thought I was asleep.
She was holding an old photo. The two of us at the beach. The sky in it was cut out.
MUM (very softly): I remember it too, Haru. It was so warm.
Above the roof, something turned over.

ENDING
In the morning, there was one cup on the table.
On the floor by my bed, there was a shadow. Sitting. Like someone holding a photo.
I asked the neighbours about my mother. They looked at me kindly.
NEIGHBOUR: Haru, sweetheart. You've always lived alone.
I still have the photo. There's a hole where the sky was.
And a space next to me where somebody used to stand.
I still remember her.
I walked into the middle of the street. And I screamed it.
HARU (screaming): I REMEMBER THE SUN! I REMEMBER MY MOTHER! I REMEMBER MY MOTH...
(curtains twitch; doors crack open; faces peek out in shock, hands over their mouths)
(before she finishes, the black sky pours down onto her like falling smoke; the groan; one beat of total black)
(silence. The street again. Where Haru stood: a black shadow burned into the stones, like ash. A girl, looking up.)
(the faces disappear back inside; doors close one by one; a neighbour walks past the shadow without looking down)
(loop to the hook shot)
